The career, in order

Every job on this list taught me something I now install for clients.

  1. 2002 to 2007 · Sydney

    Apprentice, then qualified lithographic offset printer

    Direct Impressions. Certificate III in Printing Machining and Graphic Arts. Ran the shop when the director was away, in my twenties, which is where I first learnt that a business is a set of jobs that have to flow, not a pile of tasks.

    What it left me with: respect for process, deadlines that can't move, and getting it right the first time because the paper's already paid for.

  2. 2007 to 2009, then on contract

    Installations Manager, Exercise Australia; then TechFit

    Fitted out the equipment in 16 Anytime Fitness gyms around the country, plus clubs for Orangetheory, Snap, Jetts, Crunch, BodyShape and independents. Ran four installation crews and their vans. When Exercise Australia went under in the GFC, the operations manager started TechFit and I worked with him between jobs.

    What it left me with: scheduling crews across states, delivering to a hard opening date, and the hundred small handovers that make or break a fit-out.

  3. 2009 to 2012 · Sydney's Northern Beaches

    Travel Consultant to Store Manager, Flight Centre Group

    Started on the floor at Flight Centre Warringah Mall, Assistant Manager at Warriewood, then Manager of Escape Travel Warringah Mall: a team of eight, about 60 holidays a month, and one of the group's top-selling consultants.

    What it left me with: running a retail shopfront with staff, targets, rosters and a booking system. It's the same shape as a salon, a clinic or a studio.

  4. 2013 to 2020 · Mackay, Queensland

    Built Anytime Fitness Mackay from the ground up, and owned it outright

    Found the site, handled the lease, council and DA approvals, then ran the build myself: builders, chippies, sparkies, concreters and every other trade, with my own hands on the tools to keep costs down. Brought a brand to a town that had never heard of it and ran the club for seven years. Part-owned the Mount Isa and Kalgoorlie clubs along the way.

    What it left me with: what it costs when a variation isn't written down, what a trade does when nobody's coordinating, and how an owner ends up as the message relay for everything.

  5. 2014 to 2026 · Queensland coal mines

    Twelve years operating the biggest machines in the country

    Started in drill and blast, then Hail Creek, Peak Downs, South Walker Creek, Foxleigh, Daunia and Meandu. Excavators from 350 to 550 tonnes, dozers, dump trucks, autonomous-haulage sites. Multi-skilled, permanent and contract.

    What it left me with: systems that are followed because people's lives depend on them. Pre-starts, take-fives, procedures, handovers. That discipline is what I bring to a contractor's paperwork.

  6. 2021 to 2026

    Mine Emergency Response Team: fire and rescue

    Emergency Response Team member at Foxleigh and Meandu. Certificate III in Emergency Response and Rescue. Advanced resuscitation, oxygen therapy and pain management, kept current.

    What it left me with: calm when it's going wrong, and the habit of rehearsing the plan before you need it.

  7. 2020 to now

    Finance broking, and XLCR

    Ran a finance broking business, with all the compliance, documentation and client-file discipline that comes with it. XLCR Pty Ltd was registered in 2021 and is now where all of the above gets put to work for other small businesses.

How I work

On site. With the crew.

Discovery is silent: day one I work or shadow and watch how the work actually flows. I audit from what I've seen, not what I've been told. I fix three things, not thirty. I build in the tools you already run, and I make sure the foreman can use it from a phone, or it won't last a fortnight.

Then I measure: owner hours on admin, days from job close to invoice, days to get paid, "where is X?" calls. Before and after. That's how you know it worked.
